# #english-note 
## nouns
can be singular or plural
some are only singular and some are only plural 
nouns can be masculine, feminine, neuter, common (either masculine or feminine depending on the sex)
male beings, rivers, winds, months and mountains are generally masculine
female beings, cities, countries, plants, trees, gems, many animals (especially birds) and most abstract qualities are typically feminine
indeclinable nouns, infinitives, terms or phrases used as nouns, and word quoted merely for their form are neuter
### cases
#### nominative
used for the subject of a sentence
always end in a for plural neuter nouns (also true for greek ones)
#### genitive
used for possession
#### dative
used for indirect object
#### accusative
used for direct object of a verb or with some prepositions
always the same as the nominative for neuter nouns
#### ablative
used with some prepositions
used for: from with in at by
#### locative
accusative/ ablative of location without the preposition
retained only for some town and a few other words
#### vocative
used for direct address
always the same as the nominative except non neuter nouns in -us of the second declension and some greek nouns
#### obliques cases
any case other than the nominative or vocative

## verbs

can be in active or passive voice
can be in the indicative, the subjunctive, the imperative or the infinitive mood
can be in the :
present, imperfect, future (continued action)
perfect, plueperfect, future perfect (completed action)
the subjunctive has no future or future perfect
the imperative has only present and future
the infinitive has present, perfect and future
can be in the first, second or third person
can be singular or plural
verbs passive in form but active or reflexive in meaning are deponent
there are 4 participles: present active, future active, perfect passive and gerundive (future passive)
the gerund is used in the oblique cases in a similar way to the infinitive
the supine is used in the accusative after verbs of motion to express purpose and in the dative or ablative after adjectives

## particles
### adverbs
adverbs are regularly formed from adjectives
first and second declension: **-ē**
third declension: **-ter**
neuter accusative of adjectives and pronouns often used as adverb
ablative singular neuter (or less comonly feminine) of adjectives sometime used as adverb
a phrase or short sentence sometimes grow into an adverb
adverbs can be of place, of time, of manner, of degree, of cause, interrogative or negative
comparative: neuter accusative of the comparative of the adjective
superlative: formed from the superlative of the adjective
### prepositions

### conjunctions
most conjunctions are connected with pronominal adverbs, which cannot always be referred to the original case-forms
they connect words, phrases or sentences
coordinate conjunctions connect coordinate or similar constructions
- copulative or disjunctive (implying a connection or separation)
- adversive (implying a connection of words but a contrast of thought)
- causal (introducing a cause or reason)
- illative (denoting an inference)
subordinate conjunctions connect subordinate to what it depends on
- conditional (denoting a condition)
- comparative (implying comparison as well as condition)
- concessive (denoting a concession or admission)
- temporal 
- consecutive (expressing result)
- final (expressing purpose)
- causal (expressing cause)

## formation of words
### derivation of nouns
#### noun of agency
agent or doer of an action:  suffix **-tor** (**sor**) (m.) or **-trīx** (f.)
describe a character:  **t-** added to verb stem makes noun in **-es** (**-itis**, **-etis**)
person employed in some art or trade: **-ō** (gen: **-ōnis**)
#### names of action and abstract nouns
added to roots: **-or,-ōris** (m.), **-ēs,-is** (f.) **-us,-eris/-oris** (n.)
added to roots or verb stems: **-iō,-iōnis** (f.), **-tiō (-siō),-tiōnis (-siōnis)** (f.), **-tūra (-sūra),-tūrae (-sūrae)** (f.), **-tus,-tūs (sūs)** (m.)
acts or means and results of acts: **-men** (n.), **-mentum** (n.), **-mōnium** (n.), **-mōnia** (f.)
means or instrument: **-bulum**, **-culum**, **-brum**, **-crum**, **-trum**
abstract nouns: **-ia (-iēs)**, **-tia (-tiēs)**, **-tās**, **-tūs**, **-tūdō**
offices and groups: **-ium**, **-tium**
abstract nouns: **-nia** (f.), **-nium** (n.), **-lium** (n.), **-cinium** (n.)
### derivation of adjectives
#### nominal adjectives
diminutive adjectives: **-ulus (-a, -um)**, **-olus** (after a vowel), **-culus**, **-ellus**, **-illus**
descent or relationship: **-adēs**, **-idēs**, **-eus** (m.), **-ās** (f.), **-is** (f.), **-ēis** (f.)
full of, prone to: **-ōsus**, **-lēns**, **-lentus**
provided with: **-tus**, **-ātus**, **-ītus**, **-ūtus**
made of, belongning to: **-eus**, **-ius**, **-āceus**, **-īcius**, **-āneus (-neus)**, **-ticus**
pertaining to: **-ālis**, **-āris**, **-ēlis**, **-īllis**, **-ūllis**
belonging to: **-ānus**, **-ēnus**, **-īnus**, **-ās**, **-ēnsis**, **-cus**, **-acus (-ācus)**, **-icus**, **-eus**, **-eius**, **-icius**
place and time: **-ter (-tris)**, **-ester (-estris)**, **-timus**, **-nus**, **-ernus**, **-urnus**, **-ternus (-turnus)**
belonging to: **-ārius**, **-tōrius (-sōrius)**

#### verbal adjectives
quality or tendency: **-āx**, **-idus**, **-ulus**, **-vus (-uus, -īvus, -ūvus)**
passive qualities: **-ilis**, **-bilis**, **-ius**, **-tilis (-silis)**
participial: **-ndus**, **-bundus**, **-cundus**
